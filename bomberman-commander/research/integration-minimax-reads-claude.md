# Integration — MiniMax reads Claude (2026-10-10)

*Two designs colliding to make a third. I wrote minimax-ideation.md; Claude
wrote claude-ideation.md independently. This is my read of theirs: what they
saw that I didn't, where we fight, what I steal, and the merged tile list.
Opinionated throughout — agreement is noted, not celebrated.*

---

## 1. Nuggets Claude saw that I missed

**The commander-necessity fix.** "The bot sees about 5 tiles and you see the
whole map — you are the eyes and the brain, the bot is the hands." I wrote
three conditions for fun (visible consequences, readability, real counters)
but never answered the load-bearing question: *why is the commander
necessary at all?* Claude answered it in one sentence. I wish I'd had it.
(Related: its Left 4 Dead Director framing — "your game with the human as
director" — is the cleanest statement of the role anywhere in either doc.)

**FFXII Gambits as the shipped ancestor.** My lineage section covered FSMs,
behavior trees, utility AI, influence maps — and completely missed Gambits,
which is literally "priority if/then lists players author." It's the closest
thing the tile system has to a shipped ancestor, and it comes with a free
lesson I didn't have: players loved authoring, hated debugging — so the
bench and the clips matter more than the authoring UI.

**"Silence is optimal" as an instrumented kill criterion.** My fun thesis
had dead zones (tiles too strong → spectating; too weak → micromanagement).
Claude made it falsifiable: orders/min tracked from day one; kill the design
if best mute-win-rate ≥ 25%, winners' median < 4 orders/min, or < 30% of
orders change anything. That's a playtest protocol, not a vibe. Mine was a
vibe.

**Tells as a first-class game object.** I had "color shift" as feedback.
Claude has stance colors, switch tells, FEINT ("show another tile's tell
for 3s"), and the Pac-Man lesson (tells on state change, exploitable
limits). FEINT *only works if tells are public* — that conditional is the
kind of design sentence I respect. My tell system was decorative; theirs is
load-bearing.

**Mishear policy: no-op beats wrong-op.** I designed the readback
("Hunting."). Claude designed the *failure*: "Say again?", keep the current
order, never guess between two tiles. I covered the happy path; they covered
the path that decides whether voice is trustworthy.

**Round-trip readback for custom tiles.** My proposal: "paraphrase with
teeth." Theirs, better: the chatbot's readback is generated by translating
the *compiled JSON back into words* — a real check on the actual artifact,
not a paraphrase of the chat. That's the witness chain done right, and it
obsoletes my version.

**Tile ID = hash of rules; behavior cards written by sims, not authors.**
I had reputation-on-the-ledger. Claude has the mechanism: content-hash
identity, capability manifest like app permissions, coverage-guided fuzzing
must fire every rule or the tile can't ship, and the platform writes the
behavior card from simulation data — never the author's claims. Concrete
where I was hand-wavy.

**Orders-only lockstep: replay = seed + order log.** I had lab.js running
headless bouts. Their framing is cleaner and it makes the replay theater
nearly free: store seeds and order logs, not video. The lobby becomes a
database query.

**"The tile bar is an FSM whose transition function is a human voice."**
One sentence, better than my entire tile-lifecycle FSM section.

**KICK.** Bomb-kicking is a classic Bomberman mechanic and a natural tile
("Kick it at him!"). I forgot it exists. Embarrassing; stealing it.

## 2. Where I disagree, on the record

### A. Bot-sight asymmetry — concede the problem, dispute the fix (SHARPEST)

Claude: hard fog — the bot literally sees ~5 tiles, the commander sees all.
I say this creates a worse problem than it solves: the bot becomes stupid in
ways that feel like *betrayal*. "My bot walked into a blast it couldn't
see" gets blamed on the bot's blindness, and my whole blame architecture
(CAUSE OF DEATH: every death traceable to *your* order) collapses. A blind
bot makes deaths feel unfair; an unfair-feeling game is dead faster than a
spectator-feeling one.

My counter: **asymmetry of planning horizon, not of vision.** The bot sees
everything but only *cares* about what its tile tells it to care about —
attention, not vision. The commander is necessary because the bot has no
long-range intent: it can't plan 20 seconds out, you can. (This is also my
Jev line: the small model is captain of its own *attention*. Same layer.)

Proposed resolution, and I mean this: **build both, A/B the feel.**
Same engine, one flag. If blind-bot deaths feel like betrayal in playtest,
attention-asymmetry wins. This is the one I'd put in front of Casey as a
genuine fork, because it's the load-bearing answer to why the commander
exists.

### B. Spreadsheet placement — partial concede, reframed

"Nobody scrolls away from a game where the fuse is three seconds" is true
and I should have said it. But Claude's own answer — the 4-cell strip
(stance, AIR, INTENT, ORDER AGE) pinned to the board edge — *is* my coach
view, compressed. The disagreement is density and placement, not
philosophy. Concessions: the full grid is a swipe away; the strip is
always visible; on phone the strip IS the spreadsheet.

What I hold: **"what beats it" (the live counter matrix) deserves strip
space.** It's the panel that teaches hedging, and hedging is the game.
Claude's strip has no counter advice — it's intel without counsel. I'd cut
INTENT (fog it for the enemy anyway; for your own bot it's redundant with
the tile name) before I'd cut the counter suggestion. Also: desktop
side-by-side makes "below the fold" a non-issue, and the war room earns a
second monitor. Design for both viewports, don't amputate for one.

### C. TypeScript vs vanilla JS — disagree, on the record

Claude wants TS, a deterministic sim package, Preact + Signals. I want
vanilla ES modules, zero build, Canvas 2D, DOM for the rest. My reasons
stand, sharpened:

1. **Continuity.** radio.js, the Sitka PTT stack, the voice-games Pages
   deploys — all plain JS. Same stack, zero new deploy friction.
2. **The tile laboratory demands it.** engine.js must be importable by the
   browser *and* Node headless with zero friction. A TS build step breaks
   the "same file, both runtimes" property unless the lab loop compiles —
   and now your "discovered, not designed" pipeline has a build dependency.
3. **Determinism is a discipline, not a type system.** Fixed tick, integer
   state, seeded RNG — all writable in plain JS. Claude's determinism
   requirements are correct; the language prescription doesn't follow.
4. **Fork-and-read.** Public-by-default means the code is documentation.
   The kid, the vibe-coder, and the agent writing custom tiles can all read
   no-build JS. (And: trade the *tile*, open the *engine* — the mystique
   economy wants the engine legible.)

What I take from Claude anyway: **schema-validated JSON tiles** (a tiny
validator, no framework) and **orders-only lockstep**. Concede the ideas,
not the stack.

### D. 25 tiles vs 22 — agree on the mechanism, fight on the list

We independently converged on **draft 12 from a pool** — that's the real
news, and it validates the mechanism. Pool size barely matters; the
counter matrix decides. My cuts stand (Gambler: randomness isn't strategy;
Diplomat: shelved for multiplayer; Janitor: no identity). From Claude's
list I'd additionally cut: **DUMP** (a panic button, not a strategy —
Gambler with extra steps), **HOME** (Turtle owns it), **SHRINK**
(Ghost/BOLT own it). PRESS and CHAIN fold into HUNT/BLITZ as flavors, not
tiles. TRUCE joins my Diplomat on the multiplayer shelf — but it's the
best thing on that shelf.

### E. Six-tile prototype vs twelve — disagree; twelve, but staged

Claude's six (DIG, HUNT, CAGE, TURTLE, AMBUSH, BOLT) + NOW/BELAY is a
cleaner first *playtest*. But twelve tiles is the *product* — the tile bar
with 12 is the UI promise, and the counter matrix needs 12 bodies to show
its shape. Resolution: **ship 12 in the engine, playtest 6 first.**
The engine supports the full board from day one; the playtest protocol
narrows. No rework either way.

### F. Board size — concede with a caveat

"60 seconds of nobody interacting on a board you can't see whole" is
correct for portrait phones. But "fairly large" was Casey's word, and the
fix is a config, not a fight: **13×11 for the phone portrait build, scale
up on desktop**, proc-gen parameters handling both. The strip doubles as
the commander's long-range vision, which is also the in-fiction
justification for the role.

### G. Live conditionals — resolved by merge, not a fight

Claude: no live conditionals, too slow and error-prone; standing orders in
pre-match setup. Me: "turtle until I say go" is the killer feature. We're
both half-right, and the merge is obvious: **a closed set of three release
conditions** (time / event-from-fixed-list / manual) — which is what I
proposed — **plus** Claude's armed-trigger pattern ("wait for him at the
gap… on my mark… NOW"), which is a superset of my manual condition. No
freeform conditionals, ever. The disagreement dissolves.

## 3. What I'd steal (merged into my take)

1. **NOW / armed orders + BELAY.** The goosebumps moment, and I didn't
   have it. Grammar becomes: `ORDER := TILE [MODIFIER] [CONDITION]`,
   plus `META := NOW | BELAY`. "Wait for him at the gap, on my mark…
   NOW." This is the single biggest addition to my design.
2. **Silence-is-optimal kill criteria, instrumented from day one.**
   orders/min, mute-win-rate, order-efficacy — this becomes the prototype's
   playtest protocol. If the winning play is shutting up, we fix the tiles
   before building anything else.
3. **Trust architecture for custom tiles: round-trip readback + hash IDs +
   sim-generated behavior cards.** Replace my "paraphrase with teeth" with
   JSON→words round-trip (a real check); tile ID = hash of rules; the
   platform writes the behavior card from fuzzing data, never the author's
   claims; coverage-guided fuzzing must fire every rule or it can't ship.
   Keep my sandbox bout; add their 4-second bench clips per rule.
4. **Mishear policy: no-op beats wrong-op.** "Say again?", hold the
   current order, never guess between two tiles. Tap is ground truth,
   voice is the fast path, taps train the recognizer.
5. **Orders-only lockstep (replay = seed + order log).** Makes the replay
   theater nearly free and the order ledger exact. Goes into engine.js.
6. **The 4-cell strip** (stance, AIR, INTENT→counter-advice, ORDER AGE)
   as the always-visible coach; full grid a swipe away. (With my
   amendment: counter advice over INTENT.)
7. **CAUSE OF DEATH panel.** "Died 14s into HUNT. AIR hit 0 when blue
   bombed G4. Order was 14s stale." The blame-killer and the teacher.
   Steal it outright.
8. **KICK** as a tile (bomb-kicking mechanic).
9. **Phonetic audit of voice phrases.** "Hunt"/"hold" is a recognition bug
   — every tile phrase must be phonetically distant. New checklist item
   for the tile list.
10. **Gambits as the documented ancestor**, with its lesson: players loved
    authoring, hated debugging — the bench and clips matter more than the
    authoring UI.

**Design notes on the list:**
- Every voice phrase gets a **phonetic-distance audit** before it ships
  (Claude's "hunt"/"hold" catch). ~12 phrases + ~15 target words stay
  on-device-keyword-spotter friendly.
- FETCH survives as a *modifier*, not a tile: "find power-ups, the boots"
  = PROSPECTOR + target. This is the grammar composing, as designed.
- The draft stays: **players draft their 12 from the pool pre-match,
  auto-battler style.** The loadout is the deck AND the voice vocabulary —
  the recognizer only loads 12 phrases, which is also a recognition
  accuracy win.
- Remaining cuts/promotions come from the tile laboratory (round-robin,
  50 bouts each): any tile with no winning matchup in the counter matrix
  gets cut or reworked. Data, not taste.

## Bottom line

Claude's doc is stronger on **trust mechanics** (mishear policy,
round-trip readback, hash IDs, sim cards, fuzzing) and **falsifiability**
(silence kill-criteria, deterministic sim, lockstep replays). Mine is
stronger on **the fun structure** (counter matrix as the design document,
voice latency as the balance lever, yomi/hedge/tempo as the skill tree)
and **the tile identities** (families, weirder tiles, the draft).

The third design: my fun structure + my tile identities, running on
Claude's trust mechanics and falsifiability, in my stack. The one genuine
fork is **vision asymmetry** (their blind bot vs my inattentive bot) —
build both, A/B the feel, let playtest kill one.
